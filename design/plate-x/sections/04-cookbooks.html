04 · The cookbooks, opened

Step 1: the spines, close up

What the Cookbooks tier opens. The shelf comes up large. The books stand at the left, against the side wall, held by a bookend; the rest of the shelf is room for more. Each is a sewn book with a rounded cloth spine and two raised bands; the title is stamped up the cloth, and the count of recipes sits small at the foot. From above you see the cloth's top edge, a small headband, the boards' thin edges either side, and the leaves running back to the fore-edge. Click a spine to open that book. New cookbook, under the shelf, adds a blank book to the row.

Step 2: the book, at its cover

The cover first. A spine opens the book closed, cover up: cloth spine, printed paper board, the title and a small printed plate. Click the cover or the round arrow to open it at the contents. Sharing a book is on the Later list.

Step 3: the editor, over the kitchen

This is the editor. The developer's working view: the book opened over the dimmed kitchen, with the find bar and Close at the top and the strip below. Here she adds, takes out and orders recipes.

Left page: contents. The recipes in the order she sets, with the date each was added and its page. Drag the six dots at the left of a line to reorder. Each name turns to its page. The x takes a recipe out of this book only.

Right page: one recipe in full. The thread of the sewing shows in the gutter, and the folded gatherings show at the head of the spine. Turning pages. The plain round arrows beside the book, the same as the notebook and recipe box. The strip. Add a recipe and New cookbook.

The finished book

For reading through. The same book once it is made: full screen, no kitchen and no find bar, lying on a plain linen cloth. The round arrows turn the pages; the red X, top right, goes back, the same as the recipe box's full-screen card. No x, no dots, no strip, nothing to edit.

Who can do what. Developers make cookbooks: their own books on the shelf, New cookbook, and the editor. A home cook cannot make one, but can read cookbooks (and later save other developers' books; browsing them is on the Later list). Her shelf holds the sample Weekend Bakes. A spine opens the cover, then the finished book; there is no editor, so in the Home cook plan Step 3 shows the finished book. No paid tag on reading. The only paid thing is making a book: where the developer has New cookbook, she sees the yellow "paid plan" tag and "See the paid plan". A guest is the same as a home cook; that button reads "Sign up".

No open questions on this view.
