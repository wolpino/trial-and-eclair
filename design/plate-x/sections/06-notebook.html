06 · The Trials notebook, opened

The notebook, open at Croissants trial 4

Tabs down the side, one per trial. Each trial has a paper tab on the edge of its first page, grouped by recipe in A to Z order. Tabs for the pages before you stick out on the left, the rest on the right, the way a real book with tabs works. The tab you are on is white. Click any tab to jump to that trial. The orange tab at the top is the Contents.

Turning pages, two ways on one spread. Click a folded corner at the bottom of a page, or the round arrows outside the book. The words under each arrow say where it goes: back to Croissants trial 3, on to Galette trial 1.

Publish is a stamp. At the bottom of the trial's last page sits a "PUBLISH" stamp. Click it to send this trial to the recipe box as a card. The strip under the book keeps New trial, New recipe and Add a photo (it shows how many of the five photos this entry has used).

Plans. Switch the Plan to Home cook or Guest to see the read-only notebook: same sample book, pages and tabs still turn, but no stamp, no buttons to write, and the yellow "paid plan" tag tied to the top. A guest sees the same drawing; only the button changes from "See the paid plan" to "Sign up".

Contents, at the front of the book

Every recipe and its trials. Each line has the date and the page. Click a line, or a tab, to open that trial. Contents is the first page, so every trial tab is on the right and only the Contents tab is on the left. Yellow marks the trial you worked on last. Blondies trial 2 carries a small "PUBLISHED" stamp. The back arrow and back corner are gone here: there is nothing before the Contents.

Publishing a trial (Developer)

Before. A dashed "PUBLISH" stamp at the bottom of the last page, shown as it looks when pointed at.

After. Inked "PUBLISHED" with the date. The trial is now a card in the recipe box; "see the card" opens it.

A trial started from an idea. Moving "Miso caramel" off the idea board archives the idea (it leaves the board) and starts a new recipe here. Its first page says where it came from, in small writing under the title.
