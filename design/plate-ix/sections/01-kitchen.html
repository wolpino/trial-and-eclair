01 · The kitchen

The main kitchen

What changed. A 1950s fridge stands at the left edge and holds the Recently list. One plain cabinet sits between the fridge and the shelf. A small window on the right wall lets in a patch of sun. The tile is a little taller, the outlet sits inside two tiles, and there is a strip of checkerboard floor. The shelf labels are tape on the front edge of each board. The cork notes carry real idea titles.

Try it. Point at an object to see the glow and its yellow label. Click the eclair to take a bite. The third click leaves crumbs. It comes back when you reload. The Plan switch at the top shows the paid tags for a home cook.

Day and evening

Day. Pale sky, sun on the tile and counter.

Evening. Dark sky, a moon and stars, no sun patch, a warm wash from the top.

The whole fridge. These two show a little more than the kitchen does, so you can see the hinges on the far edge. In the kitchen the left part of the fridge runs off the page. The handle, trim strip, blank badge and grille stay in view.

How the Recently list is held

Magnets. One sheet, two round magnets.

Ticket rail. A steel rail with one small ticket per recipe.

Tape, fixed. One short strip across the top edge, half on the paper and half on the door.

Every name is a link. One name in each picture is shown as it looks when you point at it.

The window

Whole. The window sits fully inside the view.

Cut. A wider window runs off the page. The edge falls exactly on a glazing bar.

Shelf labels

Tape. Hand lettering on tape, stuck to the front edge of the board each row stands on.

Brass. The holders from Plate VIII.

Without. The cabinets run down to a plain base, as in Plate VIII.

With. The cabinets are a little shorter to leave room for two rows of tiles.

The narrow kitchen, for comparison

What a narrow screen loses. There is no room for the fridge, so the list is taped to the wall. The window is slimmer and the left side has two doors again.
