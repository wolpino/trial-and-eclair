A published recipe, as a visitor sees it (/r/croissants)

No account, no kitchen. This is the page a shared link opens. It reads like one page of a printed cookbook: title, picture, the short story, then ingredients beside the method. The three chips switch the measurements between as written, metric and imperial. Print gives the same page on paper. The picture here is a drawing that stands in for the one hero photo; a real photo would sit in the same ink frame.

Only one mention of the app. The footer line says where the page was made and offers one link. The small empty circle holds the place for a mark, which is not decided yet.

A published cookbook (/c/weekend-bakes)

Cover, contents, then the recipes. The top is the cover and a short description. The contents list stays on the left and each title is a link. One recipe shows in full on the right, as the copy made when it was added to the book. Previous and Next step through the book in order, at the top and again at the end of each recipe.

The recipe page on a phone

One column, same order. Title, picture and story first. Then the measurement chips, ingredients, and the method. Print and the footer come last. The type is large enough to read from a counter while baking.
